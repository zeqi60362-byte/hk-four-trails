Source. , sheet “点位指标”. One row per panorama, sampled every ~30 m along each trail.

Time windows. e_08–e_14 are mean geometric exposure over each two-hour window (08–10, 10–12, 12–14, 14–16), independent of when a walker arrives. Their average is the static share.

Canopy gaps. Visibility is not binary. Each 5° × 2° cell of the panorama is scored by its share of sky; a cell of crown counts as the point's own gap fraction (2–15 %), and any other obstruction counts as zero. Exposure is the sine of solar altitude times that transmittance, so a closed canopy gives a small positive value rather than 0. Below the terrain horizon it is still 0.

Arrival exposure. e_arr_* is the instantaneous exposure when a walker departing at that hour reaches the point at 4 km/h. It is used in the Simulation tab only; the two families are not interchangeable.

Units. Exposure runs 0–1, where 1 is direct sun at normal incidence. Multiply by 15 to get sun minutes per km at walking pace.

Sun exposure classes used in the filter apply to the selected time window: shaded < 0.05, low 0.05–0.30, moderate 0.30–0.55, high ≥ 0.55.

Elevation comes from Copernicus DSM 30 m, which includes vegetation height and therefore reads high under forest.

Image quality. “Low” marks the bottom 20% of ground-region sharpness. Low-quality points are still part of the analysis.

Obstruction angles are the maximum over 360 azimuths for tree crowns, structures and terrain; the combined angle is the 60 % skyline (the elevation above which 60 % of that direction is sky) or the terrain angle if that is higher.
